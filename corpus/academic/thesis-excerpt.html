3.1 Sampling design

The sampling design followed the stratified protocol described in the methods chapter. Six reaches were selected across three lowland catchments, each reach subdivided into five cross-sections, and each cross-section sampled at three points across the channel.Reach selection was constrained by access; the three catchments were chosen because their land use spans the range from pasture to arable. The design yields ninety sampling points per campaign, which is sufficient to estimate the suspended-sediment load with the precision reported in section 3.4.

Samples were collected during baseflow and during the falling limb of storm events, because the transport regime differs between the two and a design that samples only baseflow underestimates the annual load by a wide margin.The falling limb carries the bulk of the coarse fraction in these catchments; sampling it is the single most important methodological decision in the study.

3.2 Instrumentation

Discharge was measured at each cross-section with an acoustic Doppler current profiler, and suspended-sediment concentration was determined by filtration of depth-integrated samples. Turbidity was logged continuously at the downstream end of each reach and calibrated against the filtered samples. The instrumentation is summarized below.

3.3 Laboratory procedure

Each sample was filtered through a pre-weighed glass-fibre filter, dried at 105 degrees Celsius for two hours, and re-weighed to the nearest tenth of a milligram. The difference in mass, divided by the filtered volume, gives the suspended-sediment concentration. The procedure was repeated in triplicate for one sample in twenty as a quality check.The triplicate check is the only direct estimate of laboratory precision in the dataset; it is reported in the appendix.

3.4 Derived quantities

Suspended-sediment load was computed as the product of concentration and discharge, integrated over the measurement interval. Where the interval was longer than the sampling cadence, the load was estimated by the flow-weighted interpolation described in the appendix.
