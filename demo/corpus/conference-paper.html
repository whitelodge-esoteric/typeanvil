Sparse sampling of channel bedload: how few samples are enough?

L. Ashcroft, R. Menon, and P. Ilves · Institute of Hydrology, Caldon
Abstract. Bedload sampling in gravel-bed rivers is expensive, and the number of samples needed for a stable estimate is contested. We resample a two-year bedload dataset to estimate the minimum sample count that reproduces the annual load within ten percent, and we report how that count varies with the sampling method and the flow regime. The result is a practical rule for field campaigns.

1. Introduction

Key resultA campaign of about one hundred event-targeted samples reproduces the annual bedload load within ten percent.

Bedload is the coarse fraction of sediment transport and the fraction most often ignored, because it is the hardest to measure. A Helley-Smith sampler deployed at a cross-section returns a noisy estimate, and the annual load is the sum of many such estimates. The number of samples that makes the sum stable is the practical question this paper addresses.

2. Data

The dataset comprises 1,240 bedload samples collected across three gravel-bed reaches over two years. Discharge was measured simultaneously, so each sample can be placed in its flow context. The reaches span a fifteen-fold range in median discharge.

3. Method

For each reach we drew random subsets of increasing size from the full sample set and computed the implied annual load. The subset size at which ninety percent of draws fell within ten percent of the full-set load was taken as the minimum sample count.The resampling used a fixed seed so the result is reproducible; the seed and the draw count are recorded in the supplement.

5. Discussion

The practical rule is that a campaign should target high-flow events rather than spread samples evenly. A hundred samples concentrated on events gives the same stability as several hundred spread uniformly, and the concentration is achievable because the events can be forecast a day ahead.A monitoring rule based on forecast flow is described in the field protocol; it assumes the sampler can be deployed within a working day.

6. Conclusion

Between eighty and a hundred twenty well-placed samples reproduce the annual bedload load within ten percent. The count is set by the high-flow events, and campaigns that ignore them will under-sample the load however many baseflow samples they collect.
