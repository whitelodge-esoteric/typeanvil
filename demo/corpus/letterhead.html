Paged-media typesetting, done properly.

This letterhead demonstrates named pages and page-margin boxes: the cover page carries no running content, while the letter pages that follow show the company name top-left, the domain top-right, and a page-number-of-total footer centered at the bottom. The cover uses the page: cover property to select a distinct @page cover rule whose margin boxes are empty.

Switching between named pages is a single property — no duplicated markup, no manual page breaks. The engine resolves the named page from the element's computed page value and applies the matching rule's size, margins, and margin-box content to the fragmentainer that begins there.

Re: proposal for the typesetting evaluation

Dear Evaluator,

Thank you for the opportunity to submit Northwind Systems for the evaluation of paged-media typesetting engines. We believe the documents in this corpus demonstrate precisely the wedge that matters for professional publishing: real page geometry, running content, numbered pages, cross-referenced tables of contents, and text that is justified and hyphenated to a quality standard readers recognize from fine print.

This letter itself is a demonstration. The cover page you see before this one is a named page with its own rule; the letter pages carry running headers and a page-number-of-total footer. Every element on this page — the alignment, the justified prose, the hanging punctuation — is produced by the typesetting engine under evaluation, not by a word processor pretending to be one.

We would welcome a side-by-side comparison of our output against the incumbent on any document in the corpus. The fixtures were chosen to exercise the features that matter most to publishers: fragmentation without slicing, tables that repeat their headers across pages, running strings and counters, and typography that does not embarrass the prose.

Please do not hesitate to reach out with any questions, or to request additional fixture documents exercising features not yet covered here.
