A note on the convergence of iterative page breaking

Preprint · 2026-10-02 · Comments welcome · DOI pending

1. Summary

When a page's layout depends on the total page count, the layout must be computed more than once: the first pass supplies a provisional count and the second pass consumes it. This note observes that the iteration terminates after two passes for the class of documents whose total count does not depend on the count itself, and it gives a bound for the remaining cases.

2. The problem

Consider a document that prints page i of n in the footer, where n is the total page count. The footer's width does not affect pagination,This is the assumption that carries the proof; it holds whenever the footer lives in a margin box whose size is fixed by the page geometry rather than by its content. so the count computed from the first pass is already correct. A second pass renders the correct number, and a third would change nothing.

Observation. If the only dependence of layout on the total count is through content that does not feed back into pagination, the iteration converges in exactly two passes.

3. The general case

The interesting case is a document whose content depends on the page count in a way that changes pagination. A cross-reference that expands to a longer string on a later page, for example, can push a later line over a boundary and add a page, which changes the reference again. The iteration is then a fixed-point search, and it may cycle.

The behaviour is governed by the structure of the mapping from a candidate count to the count it produces. Three outcomes are possible, and the table lists them with the example that exhibits each.

OutcomeConditionPasses
ConvergesMapping is contractive2
OscillatesMapping has a 2-cycleBounded by cap
DivergesMapping is expansiveBounded by cap

The practical resolution for a production engine is a hard cap on the number of passes, after which the last result is used. A cap of three is sufficient for every document in our test corpus and bounds the worst case at a small constant.The corpus and the pass counts for every document are published alongside this note.

4. Relation to earlier work

The two-pass structure is described in [2], and the oscillation example is the one given in [4]. The contribution here is the observation that the common case converges in two passes, which makes the cap a safety net rather than a routine part of the algorithm.
