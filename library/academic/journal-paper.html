Stage–discharge hysteresis in a small agricultural catchment

M. Rennick1 and A. Okonkwo2
1School of Geography, Ashfield University · 2Institute of Hydrology, Caldon
Abstract We report a two-year stage–discharge record from a small agricultural catchment and show that a limb-dependent rating nearly halves the median prediction error. Keywords: rating curve · hysteresis · suspended sediment.

1. Introduction

Rating curves are the workhorse of hydrological monitoring. A stage measured at a weir is converted to a discharge by a relation fitted once and applied thereafter, on the assumption that the relation is single-valued.

2. Study site and data

The site is a 4.2 km² sub-catchment drained to a compound weir with a continuous stage recorder and a pump sampler. The record spans two years and 214 storm events with complete limbs.

3. Methods

Three models were fitted: a single power law, a limb-dependent power law, and a limb-dependent power law with a stage offset. All were fitted by least squares in log space.

4. Results

The limb-dependent models outperformed the single curve on held-out data, and the improvement was concentrated on the falling limb, where error fell from 0.118 to 0.051 m³ s⁻¹.

5. Discussion

The interpretation is physical: on the rising limb the channel is filling and the water-surface slope is steep, so a given stage passes more discharge than once the channel is full.

6. Model choice

The offset term adds little beyond the limb dependence, and the residual structure shows no remaining trend with stage, so the simpler limb-dependent model is adopted throughout.

7. Conclusion

Stage–discharge hysteresis is present and material in this small agricultural catchment. A limb-dependent rating nearly halves the median prediction error.
