Annual Operations Report

Harrowdale Group · Financial year 2026 · Prepared for the executive committee · All figures unaudited

This report summarizes the operational performance of the Harrowdale Group for the financial year 2026. It covers each of the group's four divisions in turn and closes with a consolidated view. The report is circulated to the executive committee and is the source document for the operational sections of the annual review.

Manufacturing

Manufacturing delivered a solid year against a difficult input-cost backdrop. Volume grew 4.1% while the division held its margin above plan, helped by the commissioning of the second line at the Ashfield plant in the second quarter. The remaining capacity constraint is the drying stage, which is scheduled for upgrade next year.

Energy intensity fell for the third consecutive year, and the division is now within five percent of its 2030 target. The apprenticeship program recruited thirty-eight trainees, its largest intake to date.

Distribution

Distribution grew revenue by 9.4% but the margin compressed as fuel and labour costs rose faster than the rates the division was able to pass on. The network was rationalized from sixteen sites to fourteen, and the two closed sites were sold. Service levels held at or above target throughout the year despite the disruption of the closures.

Retail

Retail had its strongest year in a decade. Like-for-like sales rose 6.8% and the margin improved as the mix shifted toward own-brand lines. Eight new stores opened and three underperforming stores closed, for a net addition of five. The online channel grew 14.2% and now represents twenty-nine percent of retail revenue.

The division's principal challenge is the cost of the store estate, and a review of the lease portfolio is under way with conclusions expected in the first quarter of the new year.

Services

Services grew revenue by 12.6% at the highest margin in the group. The division is small but strategically important, because its contracts pull through manufacturing and distribution revenue and its renewal rate is the best indicator of customer satisfaction in the group.

Consolidated performance

The group closed the year ahead of plan on revenue and slightly behind on margin, the latter entirely attributable to Distribution. Capital expenditure was within budget and the balance sheet remains comfortably within its covenants. The board is asked to note the division-level performance above and the outlook summarized in the final section.

The outlook for the new year assumes modest volume growth across all four divisions and a recovery in the Distribution margin as the fuel-cost hedges take effect. The principal risks are the pace of the input-cost recovery and the timing of the manufacturing capacity upgrade.
