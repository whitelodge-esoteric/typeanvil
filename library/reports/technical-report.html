Capacity Planning for the Render Cluster

Technical Report TR-2026-14 · Northwind Systems · Prepared by the infrastructure group · October 2026

This report models the compute capacity required to render the projected document volume for the next four quarters. It uses the observed render cost per document from the production cluster and scales it against the demand forecast from the sales plan. The recommendation is stated in the final section.

1. Workload

The production cluster renders three classes of document: statements and invoices, long-form reports, and catalogues. Each class has a different cost profile, driven by page count and by the density of tables. The observed averages over the last quarter are summarized below.

3. Demand forecast

The demand forecast applies the sales plan's growth rates to the current volume. Statements grow with the account base; reports and catalogues grow faster because they are concentrated in the expanding enterprise segment. The forecast is shown for the next four quarters.

4. Headroom and recommendation

The current cluster provides 140 CPU-hours per month of burstable capacity, which is comfortable against the forecast but leaves little room for a simultaneous peak across all three classes. The peak day in Q4 2027 is projected at 3.6 CPU-hours; with a cluster that serves three classes concurrently, the practical limit is the number of parallel renders, not the total CPU budget.

The recommendation is to add capacity only for the parallel-render dimension, where utilization is highest and where a single hot document can saturate the queue. No CPU or storage expansion is required within the forecast horizon. The infrastructure group will revisit the model when the Q2 2027 actuals are available.
